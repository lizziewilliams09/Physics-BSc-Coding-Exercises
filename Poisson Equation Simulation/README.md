# **Poisson Equation - Jacobi Method**

This project numerically solves the Poisson equation in three dimensions using the Jacobi iterative method, applied to the classic electrostatics problem of a point charge in free space.  
The solver computes the electric potential and electric field distributions, demonstrating how numerical solutions reproduce the expected analytical φ ∝ 1/r and E ∝ 1/r² behaviour.



## Background

In electrostatics, the potential φ and electric field **E** are related by the Poisson equation:

$$
\nabla^2 \phi = -\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

where:
- ρ is the charge density distribution,
- ε₀ is the vacuum permittivity.

For a point charge, the analytical solutions are:

$$
\phi(r) \propto \frac{1}{r}, \qquad E(r) \propto \frac{1}{r^2}
$$

The goal of this simulation is to verify these relationships numerically by discretising the system on a cubic lattice and iteratively relaxing the potential until convergence.



## Numerical Method - Jacobi Iteration

The Jacobi method updates each lattice point by taking the average of its six nearest neighbours and adding the local charge density term.  
The algorithm iterates until the change between successive updates (the total error) falls below a chosen tolerance ε.

The 3D discrete update rule is:

$$
\phi_{i,j,k}^{(n+1)} = \frac{1}{6} \left( 
\phi_{i+1,j,k}^{(n)} + \phi_{i-1,j,k}^{(n)} +
\phi_{i,j+1,k}^{(n)} + \phi_{i,j-1,k}^{(n)} +
\phi_{i,j,k+1}^{(n)} + \phi_{i,j,k-1}^{(n)} +
\rho_{i,j,k}
\right)
$$

Boundary conditions are fixed at zero potential (φ = 0) on all faces of the cube.  
The electric field is then computed as the finite difference gradient of the potential.



## Files and Functionality

### **`jacobi.py`**
Main solver that:
- Constructs a cubic lattice and inserts a **unit point charge** at the centre.
- Iteratively solves for the potential until convergence.
- Computes the electric field components (Ex, Ey, Ez).
- Exports:
  - `vs R.txt` - distance, electric field strength, and potential.
  - 2D cuts through the central plane (`potential_cut.txt`, `Ex_cut.txt`, `Ey_cut.txt`).

**Inputs (via console):**
- `size` → grid size (number of lattice points per dimension)
- `epsilon` → desired error tolerance

**Outputs:**
- `potential` and electric field components across the grid
- Text files for plotting and analysis



## Data Files and Plotting Scripts

### **Generated Data Files**

| File | Description |
|--------|--------------|
| `vs R.txt` | Tab-separated distance, electric field magnitude, and potential |
| `potential_cut.txt` | 2D slice of potential through the centre plane |
| `Ex_cut.txt` / `Ey_cut.txt` | 2D slices of the x- and y-components of the electric field |



### **`plot graphs of cuts.py`**

Visualises 2D cross-sections through the charge centre.

- **Electric Field Vector Plot**:  
  Quiver plot showing normalised field direction and symmetry around the point charge.  
  Demonstrates the expected radial field pattern (arrows pointing outward, uniform in all directions).

- **Potential Colour Map**:  
  Heatmap of potential distribution.  
  High potential at the centre smoothly decreasing outward, verifying φ ∝ 1/r.

### **`plot vs R graphs.py`**

Plots field and potential as functions of radial distance.

- **Potential vs Distance** - scatter plot showing numerical φ(r).  
- **Electric Field Strength vs Distance** - scatter plot showing E(r).  
- **Log–Log Fits** - automatically fits power laws to verify slopes ≈ –1 (potential) and –2 (field).  
  The slope values are displayed in the legends of the fitted plots.

## Results and Interpretation

- The electric field vectors radiate symmetrically from the central charge, as predicted by Coulomb’s law.  
- The potential map shows a smooth, spherically symmetric gradient with highest intensity near the origin.  
- Log–log fits give slopes close to:
  - –1 for potential
  - –2 for field 
  confirming the correct physical behaviour of the numerical solution.

These results demonstrate that the Jacobi method can accurately reproduce electrostatic potentials and fields when solving the Poisson equation on a discrete grid, validating both the physics and numerical convergence of the model.
